# Local planning and orchestration workflow

## What is the actual two-stage behavior?

### Takeaway
The planner already separates human approval of direction from a mandatory, conversation-independent execution specification. Approval ends Stage 1, not the planner's job; the orchestrator must receive the separate specification as its entry point. These are inspected instructions, not evidence that a live run obeyed them. — [Planner](../../agents/planner.md#L18-L46)

### Cited Findings
- Stage 1 investigates goals and constraints, distinguishes evidence from assumptions, and presents goals, non-goals, context, approach, decisions, work streams/dependencies, acceptance criteria, risks, and verification. Human-facing content uses codebase responsibilities rather than individual implementation locations unless requested. — [Planner, lines 22–28](../../agents/planner.md#L22-L28)
- The human explicitly approves the high-level plan in Plannotator after annotation/revision; dismissal is not authorization. — [Planner, lines 28–28](../../agents/planner.md#L28)
- Stage 2 is required after approval. It references the approved plan and preserves decisions, but must stand alone for an orchestrator lacking the conversation. Investigation resolves implementation ambiguity; a material change in direction returns to the user, whereas merely writing the spec does not require another high-level approval. — [Planner, lines 30–40](../../agents/planner.md#L30-L40)
- Required execution context includes objective, scope, repository/worktree, branch/base/checkpoint, authoritative documents, prerequisites, dependencies, boundaries, and revalidation of mutable state. Work items have unique IDs, outcomes, implementation/discovery targets, inputs, actionable steps, dependencies, roles, outputs, tests, and objective completion criteria. — [Planner, lines 33–35](../../agents/planner.md#L33-L35)
- Execution requirements also include parallelism and single-writer ownership, review/fix/re-review gates, escalation rules, regression guarantees, verification commands/evidence, and separate permission requirements for consequential actions. Proposed agent-definition changes require complete proposed definitions, not just a summary. — [Planner, lines 36–39](../../agents/planner.md#L36-L39)
- The planner checks work-stream coverage and spec completeness, then delivers the exact execution-spec path. It must explicitly say handoff is not ready if that separate artifact does not yet exist. — [Planner, lines 42–46](../../agents/planner.md#L42-L46)
- The orchestrator can start from a user request **or** approved spec. It resolves dependencies, sends scoped assignments to specialist agents, owns worktrees/integration, isolates concurrent implementation workers, coordinates shared infrastructure, and asks for missing evidence rather than repeating completed work. — [Orchestrator, lines 30–37](../../agents/orchestrator.md#L30-L37)

### Inferences
- This is a two-audience workflow, not simply a rough plan followed by a more detailed human review: the approval document governs direction, while the execution spec supplies operational precision. This follows directly from the separate audiences and mandatory handoff checks. — [Planner](../../agents/planner.md#L26-L45)
- Post-approval investigation is permitted and expected, but it cannot silently change product scope or decisions. An implementation discovery can therefore invalidate only affected work items pending a new human choice. — [Planner](../../agents/planner.md#L33-L40)

### Gaps
- No live planning session, historical handoff, approval record, or runtime enforcement was inspected. The findings establish the current local textual contract only. — [Inspected contract](../../agents/planner.md)
- The planner does not prescribe a canonical directory, filename, document schema, automatic orchestrator launch, or persisted approval-binding format. Its durable-path requirement leaves these choices open. — [Planner](../../agents/planner.md#L30-L46)

## What are its strengths and remaining gaps?

### Takeaway
The current contract is strong on audience separation, authorization, evidence honesty, and delegation boundaries. The largest opportunity is not adding another planning phase, but making the approved direction, executable tasks, and later verification easier to trace and revalidate consistently. — [Planner](../../agents/planner.md#L24-L45); [Orchestrator](../../agents/orchestrator.md#L32-L37)

### Cited Findings
- The planner allows delegation only to scout and researcher; the orchestrator additionally allows oracle, worker, tester, and reviewer. Planner/source-edit prohibition is in its prose; the displayed frontmatter restricts subagent delegation rather than defining a comprehensive read-only filesystem policy. — [Planner, lines 6–18](../../agents/planner.md#L6-L18); [Orchestrator, lines 6–27](../../agents/orchestrator.md#L6-L27)
- Scout is focused lookup, reports locations and uncertainty, and avoids implementation/deep design. Researcher handles sourced external questions, distinguishes evidence from inference, and leaves broad codebase lookup to scout through the parent. — [Scout](../../agents/scout.md#L14-L20); [Researcher](../../agents/researcher.md#L13-L19)
- Worker must implement exactly its assignment, verify its own change, respect parent-owned worktrees, avoid subdelegation, and report changed files, real checks, skips, evidence, cleanup, and unresolved choices. — [Worker](../../agents/worker.md#L12-L19)
- Tester independently owns assigned verification, establishes targets/prerequisites/side effects, respects shared ownership, cleans up, distinguishes implementation checks from live-system qualification, and never fixes source without authorization. — [Tester](../../agents/tester.md#L12-L19)
- Reviewer independently checks requirements and evidence without fixing source, prioritizes actionable findings, distinguishes introduced/pre-existing/policy/optional issues, and avoids routine duplicate testing. — [Reviewer](../../agents/reviewer.md#L15-L21)
- Historical evidence must not be represented as a fresh pass; approval does not imply commit, branch, publication, deployment, or destructive-action permissions. — [Planner](../../agents/planner.md#L37-L38)
- The available goal-setup skill offers durable interviews, accepted fact IDs, automated-verification preferences, and saved review results. These are capabilities of an adjacent skill, not requirements already imposed on planner output. — [Goal setup, lines 56–103](file:///home/mbv/.agents/skills/plannotator-setup-goal/SKILL.md#L56-L103); [Goal setup, lines 105–147](file:///home/mbv/.agents/skills/plannotator-setup-goal/SKILL.md#L105-L147)

### Inferences
- Role separation makes the current design well suited to spec-driven execution: workers get bounded assignments, testers record outcomes, and reviewers assess against requirements. However, independence and invocation still depend on orchestrator instructions and each spec's gates, rather than an inspected mandatory state machine. — [Orchestrator](../../agents/orchestrator.md#L30-L37); [Planner](../../agents/planner.md#L35-L36)
- Unique work-item IDs are required, but stable requirement IDs and an explicit requirement→task→test/evidence matrix are not. Adding such a convention could close traceability gaps without exposing implementation detail in the human review. — [Planner](../../agents/planner.md#L26-L38)
- The orchestrator's acceptance of direct user requests is legitimate flexibility, not proof that all execution is approval-gated. A stronger spec-driven mode would need an explicit entry validation rule rather than assuming planner constraints automatically apply everywhere. — [Orchestrator](../../agents/orchestrator.md#L30); [Planner](../../agents/planner.md#L28-L45)
- An approval record bound to a reviewed document version/checkpoint would reduce ambiguity when an approved plan or implementation baseline changes. Existing instructions require referencing the plan and revalidating mutable state, but do not define version binding or freshness rules. — [Planner](../../agents/planner.md#L33-L40)
- Full proposed agent definitions are a useful special-case completeness safeguard. A future template should preserve this requirement rather than replacing it with task summaries. — [Planner](../../agents/planner.md#L39)

### Gaps
- No explicit schema validation, dependency-cycle check, task status lifecycle, completion manifest, change-control log, deterministic resume protocol, or spec revision compatibility mechanism is defined in the inspected agent documents. These remain possible conventions, not installed features. — [Planner](../../agents/planner.md); [Orchestrator](../../agents/orchestrator.md); [Worker](../../agents/worker.md)
- Tool availability, actual model resolution, plugin loading, and runtime permission enforcement were not verified; broad configuration and potential secrets were intentionally not read. The displayed agent definitions alone do not prove deployment state. — [Agent definitions](../../agents/planner.md#L1-L16)

## What compatibility requirements and artifact structures are plausible?

### Takeaway
Preserve the existing two-stage boundary and specialist roles. A compact durable package can add explicit approval provenance, task/test traceability, and execution evidence without changing what the human is asked to approve. The structures below are proposals, not existing files or implementation instructions. — [Planner](../../agents/planner.md#L26-L46); [Project presentation rules](../../AGENTS.md#L34-L40)

### Cited Findings
- Repository rules require keeping documents in the operating directory rather than `/tmp`, separating detailed agent specs from high-level human plans, opening review artifacts with `--tailscale`, adding `--gate --json` for approval, starting in the background, and immediately sharing the exact printed tailnet URL. HTML defaults to dark mode. — [AGENTS.md, lines 1–3 and 34–40](../../AGENTS.md#L1-L40)
- Plannotator distinguishes plain annotation from approval gates. JSON decisions are `approved`, `dismissed`, or `annotated`; process success without strict flags does not itself prove approval. Strict gate flags can persist an atomic result and distinguish approval, nonapproval, and startup failure. — [Plannotator reference, lines 42–48](file:///home/mbv/.claude/skills/plannotator/SKILL.md#L42-L48); [Gate contract, lines 73–119](file:///home/mbv/.claude/skills/plannotator/SKILL.md#L73-L119)
- The generic annotation command says to run foreground and relay stdout; it agrees that closing is not approval. Its launch instructions differ from this project's background/tailnet review rules. — [Annotation command](../../commands/plannotator-annotate.md#L5-L9); [Project rules](../../AGENTS.md#L38)
- The core Plannotator reference prefers a dedicated review tool when available and otherwise gives CLI flows. It also cautions that automatic plan-exit hooks and explicit file-based approval are different routes; do not launch duplicate review sessions. — [Plannotator reference, lines 12–25](file:///home/mbv/.claude/skills/plannotator/SKILL.md#L12-L25); [Annotation guidance](file:///home/mbv/.claude/skills/plannotator/SKILL.md#L79-L81)
- Goal setup's existing package uses `goals/<slug>/interview.json`, `interview-result.json`, `facts-review.json`, `facts-result.json`, `facts.md`, `facts.meta.json`, `plan.md`, and `goal.md`. Markdown is authoritative human-readable output; JSON preserves iteration/provenance. — [Goal setup, lines 8–22](file:///home/mbv/.agents/skills/plannotator-setup-goal/SKILL.md#L8-L22); [Facts outputs, lines 139–145](file:///home/mbv/.agents/skills/plannotator-setup-goal/SKILL.md#L139-L145); [Goal output, lines 168–181](file:///home/mbv/.agents/skills/plannotator-setup-goal/SKILL.md#L168-L181)
- Goal setup's plan currently includes ordered file/system steps and concrete checks, gates that plan, and then points `goal.md` to it. It does not itself require a distinct post-approval orchestrator spec. — [Goal setup, lines 149–181](file:///home/mbv/.agents/skills/plannotator-setup-goal/SKILL.md#L149-L181)

### Inferences
- Compatibility invariants: maintain explicit approval of direction; require a separate execution entry point after approval; preserve no-implementation planner behavior, complete proposed agent definitions, independent verification/review roles, parent-owned worktrees, shared-target mutation ownership, and separate consequential-action permissions. These are the existing contract to retain rather than reimplement under different names. — [Planner](../../agents/planner.md#L18-L46); [Orchestrator](../../agents/orchestrator.md#L32-L37); [Worker](../../agents/worker.md#L16-L19)
- Plausible minimal package under a project-owned `plans/<slug>/`: `plan.md` (human direction), `approval.json` (decision plus reviewed-version reference), `execution-spec.md` (authoritative orchestrator entry), and optional `evidence/` (real execution outcomes). Only the human plan and separate spec are currently mandatory concepts; names, JSON shape, and evidence directory are proposals. — [Planner](../../agents/planner.md#L26-L45); [Strict result file support](file:///home/mbv/.claude/skills/plannotator/SKILL.md#L99-L119)
- Plausible larger package: retain the minimal documents and add `requirements.md` with stable IDs, `tasks/` with self-contained briefs, `decisions.md` for changes/escalations, and a small manifest identifying current versions and status. Use one authoritative source per concern and cross-reference rather than duplicating mutable requirements across documents. This is a proposed scaling convention motivated by conversation-independent handoff, not a current repository format. — [Planner](../../agents/planner.md#L33-L44)
- Plausible goal-compatible package: preserve `goals/<slug>/` and its accepted facts/provenance, keep the human `plan.md` high-level under local rules, add `execution-spec.md`, and ensure the eventual execution entry links that spec rather than treating the goal summary or approval alone as handoff readiness. A mapping from fact IDs to work-item IDs/checks could reuse the existing fact metadata. — [Goal setup](file:///home/mbv/.agents/skills/plannotator-setup-goal/SKILL.md#L105-L181); [Planner](../../agents/planner.md#L32-L46); [Project presentation rules](../../AGENTS.md#L36)
- Detailed spec content can be organized as: execution context/checkpoint and authority; approved requirements/non-goals; dependency/ownership map; uniquely identified work items and self-contained agent briefs; verification/evidence expectations; review/fix/re-review and escalation gates; completion/reporting. This is a compatible organization of existing requirements, not a proposed change in human approval scope. — [Planner](../../agents/planner.md#L33-L44)
- Before using adjacent generic workflows here, reconcile their foreground/background, tailnet, and file-detail presentation differences against the project-specific rules. In particular, the goal skill's file-level reviewed plan cannot simply substitute for this planner's deliberately high-level approval artifact. — [Goal setup](file:///home/mbv/.agents/skills/plannotator-setup-goal/SKILL.md#L149-L166); [Planner](../../agents/planner.md#L26-L32); [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md#L34-L40)

### Gaps
- No actual Plannotator command was run, so CLI availability/version, strict gate support on the installed binary, tool-based review availability, tailnet publication, or automatic plan-exit hooks remain unverified. The compatibility findings describe inspected reference contracts, not exercised integrations. — [Plannotator reference](file:///home/mbv/.claude/skills/plannotator/SKILL.md#L12-L48)
- Choosing a canonical directory, minimal versus goal-compatible package, machine-readable schema, approval fingerprint, and orchestrator resume policy would require a later design decision. Nothing in this research authorizes editing the planner or implementing these proposals. — [Current planner's open artifact contract](../../agents/planner.md#L30-L46)
